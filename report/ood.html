Out-of-distribution evaluation

Normal vs. extreme: two different stories

"Extreme" means something different in each iteration. In Iteration 1, the extreme set is genuinely out-of-range (β up to 0.999999 vs. training capped at 0.8) and models failed badly. In Iteration 2, training itself already spans that range, so the extreme set is really just an independently-drawn resample — and the neural network shows no meaningful degradation.

Evidence-based reading: the plain MLP's mean R² is 0.715 on the normal test and 0.748 on the extreme test — higher, not lower. This is consistent with the extreme set no longer being out-of-distribution for Iteration-2 training, not evidence that the model generalizes better on harder data.

Iteration 1 — genuine extrapolation failure

9 scikit-learn models were trained only on β ≤ 0.8 (relativistic_dataset_1m.npy) and evaluated on the same 10,000-row extreme set (β up to 0.999999). This time the beta range genuinely was never seen in training.

The project's own evaluation notebook noted this directly: "excellent interpolation but poor extrapolation." Polynomial regression (degree 3/4) reached R² as low as −1.7×10⁹ on some targets; even gradient boosting and hist gradient boosting went negative on position, gamma, momentum, and both energy targets. Velocity and beta degraded far more gracefully (R² ≈ 0.45–0.50 for most models) since they stay bounded even at extreme β.
