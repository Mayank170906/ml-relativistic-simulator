The physics

1D special relativity under a constant force

The ground truth for every sample in this project comes from one governing law: dp/dt = F with p = γmv — not the classical F = ma. This page walks through the exact closed-form solution and lets you explore how β, γ, momentum and energy move as a particle approaches the speed of light.

Governing equation

For a particle of rest mass m under constant force F, momentum grows linearly in time:

p(t) = p₀ + F·t,   q = p / (m·c),   γ = √(1 + q²),   v = c·q/γ

Total and kinetic energy follow from the same momentum:

E = √(m²c⁴ + p²c²)    K = p²c² / (E + mc²)

Position is evaluated in a cancellation-free form that stays accurate at high β and small impulse:

x(t) = x₀ + c·t·(q + q₀) / (γ + γ₀)

Proper time accumulates from dτ = dt/γ, using a stable log1p rapidity-increment form to avoid cancellation when the impulse is tiny relative to the initial momentum.

Scientific scope

Included: flat Minkowski spacetime, one spatial dimension, constant rest mass, an externally applied constant 1D force, SI units.

Excluded: gravity, curved spacetime, electromagnetic-field models, radiation reaction, variable mass, quantum effects, faster-than-light / spacelike trajectories.

Numerical precision: IEEE-754 float64. States are rejected rather than clipped when they cannot be represented as strictly subluminal — the engine never silently clips to the light cone.
