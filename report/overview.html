Project overview

Two iterations, one guiding question

Can standard ML regressors and small neural networks recover exact relativistic dynamics from a synthetic simulator — and what happens when they're asked to extrapolate beyond the range they were trained on? This project answered that question twice: once with a narrow training distribution that failed under extrapolation, and once with a widened distribution that closed the gap.

In plain words
This page is a diary of the project: every experiment, in the order it actually happened, including the one that failed and what that failure taught the next attempt. New to the project? Start with the plain-English research guide first.
Why keep a timeline at all instead of just showing the final results?
Because the failed first attempt is as informative as the successful second one — seeing exactly what changed between them (the training β range) is the project's clearest lesson.
What's the difference between "Iteration 1" and "Iteration 2"?
Iteration 1 trained only on everyday speeds and broke on extreme ones. Iteration 2 trained on the full speed range from the start.
My physics engine

Built on a custom relativistic simulator

Every ground-truth number in every dataset in this project — position, velocity, β, γ, momentum, kinetic energy, total energy, proper time — comes from relativistic-simulator, a package I designed and published myself as the exact physics core of this pipeline.
